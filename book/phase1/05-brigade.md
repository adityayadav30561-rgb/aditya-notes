# The Brigade
~ The formation that fights with its battalions

## What a Brigade Is

A **Brigade** is a formation of usually **three battalions** with supporting units, about **3,000 to 3,500** soldiers. It sits between a Division and a Battalion. It is commanded by a **Brigade Commander** of the rank of **Brigadier**, helped by a small headquarters, the **Brigade HQ**, with a Brigade Major (a Major who runs operations) and staff for administration and supply.

The brigade is the smallest formation in the Army, and the lowest level at which different arms are mixed to fight together. It is also where an infantry battalion first gets artillery, engineers and tanks to help it.

Inside a division, a brigade has a place in a bigger plan. The divisional commander gives it a sector to defend or an objective to capture, and the Brigadier decides which battalion does what, where the artillery will fire and where the reserve will be.

## What a Brigade Needs

An infantry brigade of three battalions cannot fight alone for long. It needs help from many arms, most of which are *lent* to it by the division above.

- **Artillery:** a field regiment, usually in direct support.
- **Engineers:** a field company, to build bunkers and bridges and clear mines.
- **Signals:** a brigade signal company, detached from the divisional signal regiment.
- **Air defence:** a battery, to protect the brigade's key points from air attack.
- **Armour:** a tank squadron if the battle needs it.
- **Services:** supply, medical and repair detachments.

## Different Battalions in One Brigade

Yes, a brigade may contain *different kinds* of battalions. An ordinary infantry brigade has three infantry battalions, often from different regiments (for example, one from the Rajput Regiment, one from the Sikh Regiment and one from the Garhwal Rifles). This is common: the brigade is the first level at which battalions of different regiments serve side by side.

In an **armoured brigade** the mix is different: tank regiments plus a mechanised infantry battalion. In an **independent brigade** there may be infantry, tanks and guns all together.

## Independent Brigade

A regular brigade is part of a division. An **independent brigade** is not. It reports directly to a corps (or sometimes a Command). Because there is no division above it to provide artillery, engineers and signals, an independent brigade carries its **own supporting arms** and is therefore more self-contained. It is often used to hold a sector away from the main divisions, to act as a reserve, or to be lent to a division for a particular job.

## Brigade Group and the New All-arms Brigades

A **Brigade Group** is a brigade together with the supporting arms and services that it needs to fight on its own, for example its artillery regiment and engineer company, all under the Brigadier's command. The Army is also trying out **Rudra all-arms brigades** (announced in 2025, verify), which have infantry, armour, artillery, engineers and drones permanently in one formation.

A brigade is *not always* part of a division: independent brigades exist, and when a brigade is lent to another division, it goes under the new GOC for as long as it is needed.

## Role in an Operation

In an operation the brigade fights the battle in its sector. It has three jobs: to give each battalion a clear objective, to coordinate the artillery, engineers and tanks so that they all support that objective, and to keep a reserve. The Brigadier is also responsible for his brigade's supplies and its evacuation of the wounded.
