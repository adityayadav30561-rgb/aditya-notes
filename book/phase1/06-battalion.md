# The Battalion
~ The basic fighting unit of the Army and the family of the regiment

## What a Battalion Is

A **Battalion** is the basic fighting unit of the infantry. It has about **800 to 900** soldiers and is made up of a **headquarters** and **four rifle companies**, together with a **headquarters (support) company**. It is commanded by a **Commanding Officer (CO)** normally of the rank of **Colonel** (a Lieutenant Colonel commands in some cases).

A battalion is a **unit**. This means that it is self-contained: it has its own name and history, its own administration, and it can be moved from one brigade to another without losing its identity. A brigade will often hold three battalions, all from different regiments.

A battalion sits **below a Brigade and above a Company** in the chain.

## Inside the Battalion

### Battalion Headquarters

Battalion HQ is the CO's command post. It contains the **Second-in-Command (2IC)**, the **Adjutant**, the **Quartermaster (QM)**, the **Subedar Major** (the senior JCO), the signal staff and the administrative staff.

### The Companies

- **Four rifle companies** (often named Alpha, Bravo, Charlie and Delta), each of about 100 to 120 men, are the fighting heart of the battalion.
- The **Headquarters Company** (also called the Support Company) holds the battalion's heavier and specialist weapons, in separate platoons: **mortars**, **anti-tank** missiles, **medium machine guns**, **signals**, **pioneers** (who build and clear obstacles), the **Ghatak** (commando) platoon and a reconnaissance element.

### Who Does What

- **Commanding Officer:** commands, trains and disciplines the whole battalion, and looks after its welfare. He is responsible for all that it does or fails to do.
- **Second-in-Command (2IC):** the CO's deputy; looks after the administration and takes over if the CO is away or hurt.
- **Adjutant:** the CO's staff officer for discipline, ceremonial, personnel and the CO's orders.
- **Quartermaster (QM):** responsible for rations, clothing, equipment, ammunition and stores, so that the men have what they need to fight.
- **Subedar Major:** the senior JCO; the CO's adviser on the men, discipline and customs.
- **Medical Officer (RMO):** looks after the health of the battalion.

A Battalion Commander commands the whole battalion of about 800 men. A **Company Commander** (a Major) commands only his own company of about 120.

## Battalion in Operations

In an operation the battalion captures or holds ground. It fights at the tactical level, as part of the brigade's plan, with its rifle companies in front and its support company behind them, firing mortars and anti-tank missiles. It can also receive extra help from outside: a battery of artillery, a troop of tanks, an engineer section or an air defence detachment.

## Battalion and Regiment

The two words mean different things in different arms, and it confuses many people.

- In the **Infantry**, a **battalion** is the unit. A **regiment** is the family: for example the Kumaon Regiment, which has many battalions (1 Kumaon, 2 Kumaon, and so on). A regiment is not a fighting unit but the parent group that recruits, trains and cares for its battalions.
- In the **Armoured Corps, Artillery, Engineers, Signals and Air Defence**, the unit itself is called a **regiment**, and it is a single fighting unit of about the same size as a battalion.

The reason is **tradition**. The cavalry and artillery of the British days were organised into regiments; the infantry were organised into regiments *of several battalions*. The Indian Army kept the same names.

## Battalion and Regiment: Whose Companies?

Can a battalion contain companies from different regiments? In an ordinary infantry battalion, **no**: all the companies belong to the same regiment, and most of the soldiers share a common regimental identity. Within the regiment, the battalion may be of "one class" (for example, all Dogras) or "mixed class" (for example, companies of different communities in the same regiment), but all within the same regiment.

The main exception is the **Rashtriya Rifles**, the counter-insurgency force in J&K. Its battalions are formed from soldiers and officers of many infantry regiments and other arms, posted for a fixed tenure. The **Territorial Army** and the **Assam Rifles** (under the Ministry of Home Affairs) are also separate.

So: the battalion is the basic infantry unit, and it is the unit that is "added up" to form a brigade, and the brigade to form a division. A brigade holds battalions from different regiments, but a *battalion* holds companies from only one.

## Colonel of the Regiment

Every infantry regiment, and many other arms, has a **Colonel of the Regiment**. This is sometimes called the "father of the regiment", and it is an *honorary appointment*, **not a rank**. It is held by a very senior officer, usually a **Lieutenant General or Major General** (serving or retired), who belongs to that regiment.

It is easy to be confused, because "Colonel" in the title is not the rank of Colonel. A Colonel of the Regiment may be a Lieutenant General; the title simply marks him as the head of the regiment. His role is to guide the regiment on its traditions, its welfare and its good name, to speak for it at the highest levels, and to preside over its ceremonial occasions. He does not command the battalions in operations; the real commanders are the COs and the formation commanders above them.

> **Colonel of the Regiment vs. Colonel Commandant.** In the regiment of an arm such as the Infantry, the honorary head is the Colonel of the Regiment. In the corps and services (such as the Signals or the Engineers), the honorary head is called the **Colonel Commandant**. Both are honorary and held by Generals. The actual head of a regiment's centre, the person who runs recruiting and training there every day, is the **Commandant** of the Regimental Centre, usually a Brigadier.
