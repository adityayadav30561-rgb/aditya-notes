# Hierarchy, Rank and Appointment
~ Who commands what, and why rank is not the same as command

## The Complete Hierarchy

Putting everything together, the Army hierarchy runs as follows, from the top to the soldier. Each level is made of the level below it.

[[wide]]
## Who Commands What

| Level | Type | Commander | Rank | Strength |
| Army HQ | Headquarters | Chief of the Army Staff | General | The whole Army |
| Command (7) | Formation | GOC-in-C (Army Commander) | Lieutenant General | Several corps |
| Corps (14) | Formation | GOC (Corps Commander) | Lieutenant General | 2 to 3 divisions |
| Division | Formation | GOC | Major General | 3 to 4 brigades, 15,000 to 18,000 troops |
| Brigade | Formation | Brigade Commander | Brigadier | About 3 battalions, 3,000 to 3,500 troops |
| Battalion / Regiment | Unit | Commanding Officer | Colonel | About 800 to 900 soldiers |
| Company / Squadron / Battery | Sub-unit | Company Commander | Major | About 100 to 120 soldiers |
| Platoon / Troop | Sub-unit | Platoon Commander | Lieutenant, Captain or JCO | About 30 to 35 soldiers |
| Section | Sub-unit | Section Commander | Havildar or Naik | About 10 soldiers |
[[/wide]]

Of these, the **Command, Corps, Division and Brigade** are **formations**; the **Battalion (or regiment)** is the **unit**; and the **Company, Platoon and Section** are **sub-units**.

## Which Rank Commands Which Level

An easy guide to remember, from the bottom up:

- A **Lieutenant** usually commands a **platoon** (or a troop).
- A **Captain** is usually a platoon commander or company second-in-command, and sometimes commands a company.
- A **Major** usually commands a **company** (a squadron or battery in other arms).
- A **Lieutenant Colonel** is usually the second-in-command of a battalion, and in some cases its CO.
- A **Colonel** usually commands a **battalion** or **regiment** as its CO.
- A **Brigadier** commands a **brigade**.
- A **Major General** commands a **division**.
- A **Lieutenant General** commands a **corps** or a **Command**.
- A **General** is the **Chief of the Army Staff**.

## Rank, Appointment and Command

These three words are related, but they are not the same thing.

- **Rank** shows an officer's seniority: Lieutenant, Captain, Major and so on.
- **Appointment** is the *job* he is doing at the moment: platoon commander, adjutant, brigade major.
- **Command** is the *authority* that goes with certain appointments: the legal power to lead, to reward and to punish.

So rank and appointment can differ. A Major may be an **acting CO** while the real CO is away. A Colonel may hold a **staff appointment** at Army HQ with no troops at all. A Lieutenant in a platoon has command, but a Major serving as a staff officer in a headquarters does not.

### Who is immediately above whom

- The immediate superior of a **Platoon Commander** is the **Company Commander**.
- The immediate superior of a **Company Commander** is the **Commanding Officer**.
- The immediate superior of a **Commanding Officer** is the **Brigade Commander**.

## Commanding and Administering

Officers do two things for their men. **Commanding** means leading them in training and in operations: giving orders and being responsible for the results. **Administering** means looking after the things that keep them going: pay, leave, rations, discipline, stores and welfare. A good officer does both. In a battalion, the CO commands and the 2IC, Adjutant and Quartermaster help him to administer.

## Why the Army Is Hierarchical

The Army's structure is deliberately a strict hierarchy for four reasons. **Clear responsibility:** at every level there is one person who is answerable. **Quick decisions:** orders pass down fast with no argument at the time of danger. **Discipline:** everyone knows his place and duty. **Coordination:** thousands of men and many arms can act as one team, because each one has a defined role. In war, confusion costs lives, and the hierarchy prevents it.
