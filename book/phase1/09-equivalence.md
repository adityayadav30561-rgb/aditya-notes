# Equivalent Ranks of Units
~ The same level has different names in different arms

## Why the Names Differ

The Army is built on the same plan in every arm, but each arm kept its own **traditional names**. An infantry **battalion**, an armoured **regiment** and an artillery **regiment** are all *units* of about the same standing, each commanded by a Colonel. A **company** in the infantry is a **squadron** in the Armoured Corps and a **battery** in the artillery. If you know the level, you can translate the name into any arm.

> **The word "Corps" has two meanings.** The *Armoured Corps* is an **arm**, a branch of the Army, like the Infantry or the Corps of Signals. It is **not** a "Corps" in the sense of a fighting formation such as XV Corps. A formation Corps is a large body of divisions under a Lieutenant General; the Armoured Corps is a family of tank regiments. They only share the name. The same holds for the Corps of Signals, the Corps of Engineers and the Army Service Corps.

## How to Use the Equivalents

When an interviewer says "I am a captain in the armoured corps, what do I command?", the answer is a **troop of tanks** (the level that corresponds to an infantry platoon). A major in the artillery commands a **battery**, which corresponds to a rifle company. A Colonel in the Signals commands a **Signal Regiment**, which corresponds to an infantry battalion. The numbers and equipment differ, but the *level* and the *rank of the commander* are the same.

The sizes in the table above are indicative and change with equipment and re-organisation (verify).
