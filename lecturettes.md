## India–Pakistan Relations
- What is it: Two nuclear-armed neighbours, born of the 1947 Partition → Shaped by Kashmir, four wars and cross-border terrorism
- Why it matters: Security: nuclear flashpoint, active LoC → Regional: SAARC stalled → Strategic: China–Pakistan nexus, Afghanistan → Economic: defence burden, lost trade → Global: terrorism and nuclear risk
- Current status (Sept 2026): Pahalgam (Apr 2025) and Operation Sindoor (May 2025) remain the reference point → IWT: Court of Arbitration ruled the treaty still binding (31 Aug 2026); India rejected the ruling → Naval collision in the Arabian Sea (15 Sep 2026): first direct clash since 2025, diplomats summoned → Kashmir: statehood demand continues; US called J&K an important part of India (Aug 2026), Pakistan protested → Some signs of a thaw, nothing confirmed → India's doctrine: terror attack = act of war, no nuclear blackmail, "talks and terror cannot go together"
- Problems: Kashmir and state-sponsored terrorism (LeT, JeM) → Water dispute now a legal contest at The Hague → Maritime and LoC incidents, no hotline trust → China–Pakistan nexus (CPEC, UN terror-listing blocks) → Changing US–Pakistan equation → Drones, narcotics, disinformation → No structured dialogue
- Solutions: Zero tolerance for terror, strong deterrence → Military modernisation (air defence, drones, naval readiness, self-reliance) → Diplomatic pressure (FATF, UN) → Firm IWT stand, backed by legal and diplomatic strategy → Secure borders (anti-drone systems, smart fencing) → Kashmir: development, integration, statehood in due course → Crisis hotlines and maritime confidence-building measures → Backchannels open; people-to-people ties once Pakistan acts on terror
- Conclusion: India wants peace, but not at the cost of security or sovereignty → A strong, self-reliant India deals with Pakistan from a position of strength → Lasting peace depends on Pakistan ending terror

## India–China Relations
- What is it: Two ancient civilisations and Asia's largest neighbours, both nuclear-armed rising powers → Cooperation, competition and conflict together → About 3,500 km disputed LAC → 1962 war, Doklam 2017, Galwan 2020
- Why it matters: Security: two-front threat → Economic: supply-chain dependence (electronics, pharma inputs, rare earths) → Strategic: Indian Ocean, Indo-Pacific, China–Pakistan nexus → Global: the "Asian century" depends on these ties
- Current status (Sept 2026): BRICS Summit, New Delhi (12–13 Sep 2026): Xi's first India visit since 2019, Modi–Xi bilateral → SR talks: NSA Doval and Wang Yi, just before the summit → First Corps Commander talks in Arunachal Pradesh (Sept 2026) → Trade: up 7.9% to $127.7 bn (2025–26); China raising imports from India → Post-Galwan visa curbs still hit business and academic exchanges → Arunachal: India says the state of the border will reflect on the state of ties (Aug 2026) → India: border peace is the basis of ties; China: keep the boundary separate → Overall: cautious thaw, managed competition, not a full reset
- Problems: Unresolved boundary (Aksai Chin, Arunachal) → Trust deficit, salami slicing → China–Pakistan military nexus → Large trade deficit, import dependence → Investment and technology curbs → Brahmaputra dam → Tibet and Dalai Lama succession → Chinese presence in the Indian Ocean
- Solutions: Border first: disengagement, de-escalation → Strong deterrence: border infrastructure, military modernisation → Regular dialogue: SR talks, Corps Commander talks → Atmanirbhar Bharat, PLI, diversified supply chains → Calibrated economic engagement with security screening → Quad, Act East, Indo-Pacific partnerships → Brahmaputra data-sharing → Strategic autonomy
- Conclusion: India seeks a stable, mutually respectful relationship → Border peace is the foundation of everything else → Negotiate from strength: military, economy and partnerships
