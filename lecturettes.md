## India–Pakistan Relations
- What is it: Two nuclear-armed neighbours, born of the 1947 Partition → Shaped by Kashmir, four wars and cross-border terrorism
- Why it matters: Security: nuclear flashpoint, active LoC → Regional: SAARC stalled → Strategic: China–Pakistan nexus, Afghanistan → Economic: defence burden, lost trade → Global: terrorism and nuclear risk
- Current status (Sept 2026): Pahalgam (Apr 2025) and Operation Sindoor (May 2025) remain the reference point → IWT: Court of Arbitration ruled the treaty still binding (31 Aug 2026); India rejected the ruling → Naval collision in the Arabian Sea (15 Sep 2026): first direct clash since 2025, diplomats summoned → Kashmir: statehood demand continues; US called J&K an important part of India (Aug 2026), Pakistan protested → Some signs of a thaw, nothing confirmed → India's doctrine: terror attack = act of war, no nuclear blackmail, "talks and terror cannot go together"
- Problems: Kashmir and state-sponsored terrorism (LeT, JeM) → Water dispute now a legal contest at The Hague → Maritime and LoC incidents, no hotline trust → China–Pakistan nexus (CPEC, UN terror-listing blocks) → Changing US–Pakistan equation → Drones, narcotics, disinformation → No structured dialogue
- Solutions: Zero tolerance for terror, strong deterrence → Military modernisation (air defence, drones, naval readiness, self-reliance) → Diplomatic pressure (FATF, UN) → Firm IWT stand, backed by legal and diplomatic strategy → Secure borders (anti-drone systems, smart fencing) → Kashmir: development, integration, statehood in due course → Crisis hotlines and maritime confidence-building measures → Backchannels open; people-to-people ties once Pakistan acts on terror
- Conclusion: India wants peace, but not at the cost of security or sovereignty → A strong, self-reliant India deals with Pakistan from a position of strength → Lasting peace depends on Pakistan ending terror

## India–China Relations
- What is it: Two ancient civilisations and Asia's largest neighbours, both nuclear-armed rising powers → Cooperation, competition and conflict together → About 3,500 km disputed LAC → 1962 war, Doklam 2017, Galwan 2020
- Why it matters: Security: two-front threat → Economic: supply-chain dependence (electronics, pharma inputs, rare earths) → Strategic: Indian Ocean, Indo-Pacific, China–Pakistan nexus → Global: the "Asian century" depends on these ties
- Current status (Sept 2026): BRICS Summit, New Delhi (12–13 Sep 2026): Xi's first India visit since 2019, Modi–Xi bilateral → SR talks: NSA Doval and Wang Yi, just before the summit → First Corps Commander talks in Arunachal Pradesh (Sept 2026) → Trade: up 7.9% to $127.7 bn (2025–26); China raising imports from India → Post-Galwan visa curbs still hit business and academic exchanges → Arunachal: India says the state of the border will reflect on the state of ties (Aug 2026) → India: border peace is the basis of ties; China: keep the boundary separate → Overall: cautious thaw, managed competition, not a full reset
- Problems: Unresolved boundary (Aksai Chin, Arunachal) → Trust deficit, salami slicing → China–Pakistan military nexus → Large trade deficit, import dependence → Investment and technology curbs → Brahmaputra dam → Tibet and Dalai Lama succession → Chinese presence in the Indian Ocean
- Solutions: Border first: disengagement, de-escalation → Strong deterrence: border infrastructure, military modernisation → Regular dialogue: SR talks, Corps Commander talks → Atmanirbhar Bharat, PLI, diversified supply chains → Calibrated economic engagement with security screening → Quad, Act East, Indo-Pacific partnerships → Brahmaputra data-sharing → Strategic autonomy
- Conclusion: India seeks a stable, mutually respectful relationship → Border peace is the foundation of everything else → Negotiate from strength: military, economy and partnerships

## India–Afghanistan Relations
- What is it: Old civilisational ties; India the largest regional development partner for two decades (Salma Dam, Parliament building, Zaranj–Delaram highway) → Backed the Northern Alliance and the Ghani government → Taliban takeover (Aug 2021): India shut its embassy → Now pragmatic re-engagement with the Taliban regime
- Why it matters: Security: no Afghan soil for terror against India → Strategic: counters Pakistan's "strategic depth" and China's footprint → Connectivity: gateway to Central Asia via Chabahar and INSTC → Economic: market, minerals, trade → Humanitarian: goodwill among the Afghan people
- Current status (Sept 2026): Kabul mission upgraded to full embassy (Oct 2025); Afghan embassy in Delhi handed to the Taliban, first Taliban diplomat in India → Ministerial visits: agriculture minister (Jul 2026) the fourth, after foreign, health and commerce → Trade: Kabul–Amritsar and Kabul–Delhi air freight corridors reactivated (Dec 2025); trade above $1.5 bn last fiscal → Recognition: no formal recognition of the Taliban government yet → Pakistan factor: India condemned Pakistan's strikes in Afghanistan at the UN; Pakistan calls Afghanistan a "colony of India" → MEA (Aug 2026): engagement expanded through diplomacy, humanitarian aid and development assistance
- Problems: No direct land route: Pakistan blocks transit, Attari–Wagah closed since May 2025 → Chabahar under pressure from the US–Iran conflict → Taliban curbs on women and human rights → Pakistan–Afghanistan conflict spillover, Pakistan alleging an Indian hand → China's economic push in Afghanistan → Terror groups still active (TTP, ISKP, LeT/JeM links) → Taliban an unpredictable partner
- Solutions: Calibrated engagement: aid and development, no recognition until the Taliban delivers on terror and inclusivity → Expand the air corridor and Chabahar; keep the US sanctions waiver alive → Targeted projects in health, agriculture and education → Written assurances that Afghan soil won't be used against India → Coordinate with Russia, Iran and Central Asia (Moscow format) → Raise women's rights quietly through diplomacy and multilateral forums → Stay out of the Pakistan–Afghanistan fight
- Conclusion: India's policy is guided by national interest, not ideology → The Afghan people remain India's friend, whoever rules in Kabul → Safe borders, regional connectivity and a terror-free neighbourhood come first

## India–Nepal Relations
- What is it: Unique "Roti-Beti" bond; open border with five Indian states (Sikkim, West Bengal, Bihar, UP, Uttarakhand) → 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship as the bedrock → Landlocked Nepal depends on India for transit and sea access → Gorkha soldiers in the Indian Army; shared Hindu–Buddhist heritage
- Why it matters: Security: open border, Siliguri Corridor, buffer with China → Strategic: counters Chinese influence in the Himalayas → Economic: hydropower, trade, remittances → Cultural: Janakpur–Ayodhya, Lumbini, Pashupatinath → Neighbourhood First: a test case for India's regional diplomacy
- Current status (Sept 2026): Political change: Gen Z protests forced PM Oli out; Balendra Shah (RSP) government took office (27 Mar 2026) → Lipulekh: Nepal objected to its use for the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra (3 May 2026); India says it is bilateral, no third-party role; Boundary Working Group doing field work → Engagement: Lamichhane met Modi (3 Jun); FM Shisir Khanal visited India (5–7 Jun); Jaishankar met FM Swarnim Wagle (18 Sep) on economy, energy, connectivity and a "reset" → Digital and power: UPI–NPI remittance link (Jun 2026); Nepal importing 1,154 MW from India → Tone: PM Shah calls ties harmonious and strong (29 Sep 2026)
- Problems: Kalapani–Lipulekh–Limpiyadhura dispute (Treaty of Sugauli, 1816) → Nepal's demand for "sovereign equality" and a revised 1950 Treaty → Agnipath and Gorkha recruitment friction → China's growing role (BRI, grants, projects) → Anti-India "big brother" narrative in Nepali politics → Open-border security: smuggling, trafficking, fake currency → Water: Kosi floods, hydropower sharing → Political instability in Nepal
- Solutions: Settle the boundary quietly through the Boundary Working Group, no third party → People-centric diplomacy beyond the political elite → Power trade and hydropower investment (cross-border grids) → Connectivity: rail, roads, integrated check posts, airports → Digital integration (UPI, Bhashini) → Disaster cooperation (Operation Maitri-type relief, flood early warning) → Agnipath: recruitment options for Gorkhas → Respect Nepal's sovereignty, avoid heavy-handedness
- Conclusion: Ties are civilisational; borders and governments cannot break them → Treat Nepal as an equal partner, not a sphere of influence → Strong India–Nepal ties anchor stability in the Himalayas and the region

## India–Bangladesh Relations
- What is it: India's closest eastern neighbour; 4,096 km border, India's longest with any neighbour → Born of India's role in the 1971 Liberation War → Shared language, culture and 54 common rivers → Partners in BIMSTEC, BBIN and Act East
- Why it matters: Security: Northeast insurgency, cross-border terror, illegal migration → Strategic: Bay of Bengal, Siliguri Corridor, China's presence in Dhaka → Economic: India's largest trade partner in South Asia; transit route to the Northeast → Water: Ganga and Teesta → Connectivity: rail, roads, power grid, Chittagong and Mongla ports
- Current status (Sept 2026): Political change: Hasina ousted (Aug 2024); elected BNP government under PM Tarique Rahman → Hasina question: in exile in India, sentenced to death in absentia; Dhaka demands extradition; India says the request is being examined under law → Visits: Rahman yet to visit India; Aug visit fell through, skipped BRICS in New Delhi; talks on a Nov 2026 visit resumed (trade, Ganga treaty on agenda) → Tone: MEA wants "positive, constructive and forward-looking" ties (22 Sep); Dhaka wants a "reset", calls India "just another country" (13 Sep) → Water: 1996 Ganga Water Treaty expires Dec 2026; Teesta still unresolved → Irritants: border killings and push-ins
- Problems: Hasina extradition → Ganga Treaty expiry and Teesta deadlock → Border killings, push-ins, illegal migration → Safety of minorities, including Hindus → Growing Chinese footprint (Teesta project, ports, defence) → Anti-India sentiment in Bangladeshi politics → Trade deficit and non-tariff barriers → West Bengal politics complicating water deals
- Solutions: Water first: new Ganga treaty on a technical basis; move Teesta talks forward → Keep the Hasina question on a legal track, don't let it freeze ties → Resume high-level visits; use existing bilateral mechanisms → Border management: fencing, joint patrols, non-lethal methods; zero tolerance for smuggling and terror → Economic engagement: trade, power (Nepal–Bangladesh–India grid), rail and port connectivity → People-to-people: visas, medical tourism, education, culture → Raise minority safety through quiet diplomacy → Deal with Dhaka as a state, not with one party or leader
- Conclusion: Bound by geography, history and interest → The relationship must outlive governments on both sides → A stable, friendly Bangladesh is vital for the Northeast, eastern security and Act East

## India–Sri Lanka Relations
- What is it: Closest maritime neighbour, 30 km away across the Palk Strait → 2,500 years of civilisational ties (Buddhism, Ramayana, Tamil links) → Ties since 1948; 1987 Indo-Lanka Accord and IPKF a bitter chapter → Core to Neighbourhood First and MAHASAGAR
- Why it matters: Security: sits astride key Indian Ocean sea lanes; its soil must not be used against India → Strategic: counters China's Hambantota, ports and research vessels → Economic: India one of Sri Lanka's largest trade partners; key SAARC trade partner → Humanitarian: India as first responder (2022 crisis, Cyclone Ditwah) → Tamil Nadu: Tamil and fishermen interests
- Current status (Sept 2026): Defence: Rajnath Singh in Colombo (9 Sep 2026), first defence minister visit in about 38 years; 3 MoUs (L70 gun upgrades, NCC and defence college cooperation, youth exchanges) → Assurance: President Dissanayake says Sri Lankan territory will never be used against India → Cyclone Ditwah: $450 mn relief package (Dec 2025); rupee lines of credit of $250 mn for railways and $100 mn for livestock (Aug 2026); Indian Bailey bridges, Northern Railway restored → Earlier anchor: Modi's visit (Apr 2025): first defence umbrella MoU, Trincomalee energy hub with UAE, grid interconnection, over $100 mn loans turned into grants → Outreach: VP Radhakrishnan's visit (Apr 2026): 4,000 houses for estate workers, scholarships; Dissanayake at India AI Impact Summit (Feb 2026)
- Problems: Fishermen: Indian trawlers in Sri Lankan waters, arrests, bottom-trawling damage → Katchatheevu row in Tamil Nadu politics → China's footprint: Sinopec refinery at Hambantota, research vessel visits, lapsed moratorium on such ships → Tamil aspirations: 13th Amendment, provincial council elections → Nationalist backlash against ETCA and Indian dominance → Debt and economic fragility → Sri Lanka balancing between India and China
- Solutions: Fishermen: joint working group, shift to deep-sea fishing, alternative livelihoods, humane handling → Hold Sri Lanka to its security commitment; monitor Chinese ship visits → Deepen defence ties: Mitra Shakti, SLINEX, Colombo Security Conclave, training → Economic anchors: Trincomalee hub, grid link, power trade, ports, UPI, development grants → Debt support and tourism → Tamil issue: quietly encourage devolution and provincial elections → Handle Katchatheevu sensitively, no public brinkmanship → Respect Sri Lanka's sovereignty
- Conclusion: Civilisational twins with interlinked security → India must be a trusted, non-transactional partner, not a creditor power → A friendly, stable Sri Lanka secures India's southern flank and the Indian Ocean Region

## India–Bhutan Relations
- What is it: Closest and most trusted Himalayan friend; 699 km largely peaceful border → 1949 treaty, revised in 2007 to give Bhutan full say in foreign policy → Foundations laid by the Third King and Nehru; diplomatic relations since 1968 → India Bhutan's main development partner since its first Five Year Plan (1961)
- Why it matters: Security: Doklam and the Siliguri Corridor ("Chicken's Neck") near the Bhutan–China border → Strategic: stable buffer against China, which has no diplomatic ties with Bhutan → Economic: India is Bhutan's top trade partner → Energy: Bhutanese hydropower feeds India's grid → People: about 50,000 Indians work in Bhutan
- Current status (Sept 2026): Visit: Jaishankar in Bhutan (16–18 Sep 2026), met the King, PM Tshering Tobgay and FM D N Dhungyel → Projects: 54 EVs handed over, UPI for Bhutanese in India, 65-bed mother-and-child hospital in Mongar, 43 Jersey cows for Bumthang → Energy: ₹4,000 crore concessional credit line; Punatsangchhu-II (1,020 MW) exporting since 2025, Punatsangchhu-I (1,200 MW) in progress; solar and green hydrogen → Connectivity: Samrang, tenth land customs station (Aug 2026); India funding first rail links, Kokrajhar–Gelephu and Banarhat–Samtse → Gelephu Mindfulness City: India backs the King's special region on the Assam border; Joint Working Group engaged → Trade: Agreement on Trade, Commerce and Transit valid only till 2026
- Problems: China–Bhutan boundary talks (25 rounds); Chinese interest in Doklam near the Chicken's Neck → Bhutan's trade deficit; non-tariff barriers on its exports → Bhutan wants to diversify beyond hydropower → Debt and cost overruns on hydro projects → Environmental and Gross National Happiness concerns over big infrastructure → Chinese offers of formal diplomatic ties → Youth unemployment and migration from Bhutan
- Solutions: Align Indian and Bhutanese security interests on any China boundary deal; Doklam to be settled trilaterally → Renew the trade, commerce and transit agreement on liberal terms → More market access, integrated check posts and rail links → Back Gelephu Mindfulness City as a non-hydro growth engine → Wider energy partnership: solar, green hydrogen, grid integration, fair tariffs → Digital integration (UPI, RuPay); scholarships (ICCR, Nalanda) → Treat Bhutan as an equal; respect its sovereignty and choices
- Conclusion: A model of friendship between a large and a small neighbour → Trust, not treaties, holds the relationship together → A secure, prosperous Bhutan is vital for India's Northeast and Himalayan security
