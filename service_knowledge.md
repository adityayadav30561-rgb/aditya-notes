## Phase 1 – Army: Basic Organisation
- What is the primary role of the Indian Army? :: Defend India's territorial integrity against external aggression and internal threats; aid civil authority (disaster relief, law and order when asked).
- Who is the Supreme Commander of the Indian Armed Forces? :: The President of India (Smt Droupadi Murmu).
- Who is the professional head of the Indian Army? :: Chief of the Army Staff (COAS) – Gen Dhiraj Seth (verify).
- What is the rank of the Chief of the Army Staff? :: General (four-star).
- Where is Army Headquarters located? :: New Delhi (South Block / new Thal Sena Bhawan, Delhi Cantt).
- How is the Indian Army organised, and what are its major levels? :: Army HQ → Commands → Corps → Divisions → Brigades → Battalions/Regiments → Companies → Platoons → Sections.
- What is the difference between a formation and a unit? :: Formation = grouping of units under a HQ (Brigade and above). Unit = self-contained basic body like a Battalion/Regiment, commanded by a Colonel.
- What is the difference between an arm and a service? :: Arms do the fighting (Infantry, Armour, Artillery etc.); Services support and sustain them (ASC, AMC, EME, AOC etc.).
- What is the difference between a combat arm and a supporting arm or service? :: Combat arms close with the enemy (Infantry, Armoured, Mech Inf); combat support arms help them fight (Artillery, Engineers, Signals, Army Air Defence, Army Aviation, Intelligence); services provide logistics and admin.
- What is the chain of command in the Indian Army? :: President → Raksha Mantri/Govt → COAS → Army Commander → Corps Cdr → GOC Div → Bde Cdr → CO → Coy Cdr → Pl Cdr → Section Cdr.
- What is the difference between command and control? :: Command = legal authority and responsibility to direct forces; control = directing and coordinating the forces' actions to carry out the plan.
- What are the operational and administrative functions of Army Headquarters? :: Operational: plans, policy, strategy, deployment. Administrative: personnel, training, logistics, procurement, budget, welfare.

## Phase 1 – Army: Commands
- How many commands does the Indian Army have, and what are they called? :: Seven: Northern (Udhampur), Western (Chandimandir), South Western (Jaipur), Southern (Pune), Central (Lucknow), Eastern (Kolkata), Army Training Command – ARTRAC (Shimla).
- Which commands are operational? :: Six: Northern, Western, South Western, Southern, Central, Eastern. (Central is mainly a static/admin command with the central sector of the LAC.)
- Which command is responsible for training? :: Army Training Command (ARTRAC), Shimla.
- What is each command's geographical area of responsibility? :: Northern: J&K and Ladakh. Western: Punjab, Himachal, parts of Haryana/J&K border. South Western: Rajasthan. Southern: Gujarat, Maharashtra and southern states. Central: UP, MP, Uttarakhand, Bihar etc. Eastern: Sikkim, NE states, West Bengal.
- Who commands an Army Command? :: General Officer Commanding-in-Chief (GOC-in-C), the Army Commander.
- What is the rank of an Army Commander? :: Lieutenant General.
- What is the difference between an Army Command and a Corps? :: Command is the highest field HQ covering a whole region with several corps; a Corps is a fighting formation of 2–3 divisions under a Command.
- Which command is responsible for the northern borders? :: Northern Command (J&K/Ladakh); Central (Uttarakhand/Himachal sector) and Eastern for the eastern LAC.
- Which command is responsible for the western sector? :: Western Command (with South Western).
- Which command is responsible for the northeastern region? :: Eastern Command, Kolkata.
- What is the significance of Southern Command? :: Largest by area; covers the south, Gujarat and Maharashtra; looks after desert/coastal sectors; oldest command (est. 1895); HQ Pune.
- What is the role of Central Command? :: Static/admin command for the central heartland; responsible for the central sector of the LAC (Uttarakhand–Himachal).
- What is the role of Army Training Command? :: Formulates training doctrine and concepts; runs category A training establishments; HQ Shimla.
- Why does the Army need geographically divided commands? :: Borders are long and different in terrain and threat; regional commands give focused, faster command and control.
- Can the boundaries of an Army Command change? :: Yes – they are reorganised as threats change (e.g. South Western Command created in 2005).
- What is the difference between an operational command and a training command? :: Operational commands plan and fight wars in their sector; ARTRAC deals with doctrine, training and schools.

## Phase 1 – Army: Corps
- What is a Corps? :: A corps is a field formation of 2–3 divisions with supporting arms; there are 14 corps in the Indian Army.
- Where does a Corps fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Between a Command and a Division.
- Who commands a Corps? :: General Officer Commanding (GOC) of the Corps.
- What is the rank of a Corps Commander? :: Lieutenant General.
- How many divisions can a Corps have? :: Usually 2–3 divisions (plus independent brigades).
- Is every Corps organised the same way? :: No – it depends on role and terrain (strike, holding, mountain).
- What is a Corps HQ? :: The headquarters that plans and controls the corps' operations.
- What is the difference between a Corps and a Division? :: A Corps has several divisions under a Lt Gen; a Division has 3–4 brigades under a Maj Gen.
- What is a Strike Corps? :: An offensive corps built around armour and mechanised forces to strike deep into enemy territory (e.g. 1, 2, 21 Corps).
- What is a Holding Corps? :: A defensive corps that holds the border and absorbs enemy attacks.
- What is the difference between a Strike Corps and a Holding Corps? :: Strike = offensive, armour-heavy, mobile; Holding = defensive, infantry-heavy, stays at the border.
- What is a Corps-level reserve? :: Forces kept uncommitted at corps level to react to a crisis or exploit success.
- What is a Corps' role during operations? :: Plans and conducts the battle in its sector, allotting divisions and supporting arms.
- What are Corps troops? :: Units held directly under Corps HQ: artillery, engineers, signals, air defence, aviation, logistics.
- Why does a Corps need artillery, engineers, signals and other supporting elements? :: For combined-arms fighting – fire support, mobility, communication and protection.

## Phase 1 – Army: Division
- What is a Division? :: A major tactical formation of 3–4 brigades with supporting arms – about 15,000–18,000 troops.
- Where does a Division fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Between Corps and Brigade.
- Who commands a Division? :: General Officer Commanding (GOC) of the Division.
- What is the rank of a Divisional Commander? :: Major General.
- How many brigades usually make up a Division? :: Three (sometimes four).
- Is every Division the same strength? :: No – Infantry, Mountain, Armoured and Artillery divisions differ.
- What is a Division Headquarters? :: The HQ that commands and controls the division.
- What are the major types of divisions in the Indian Army? :: Infantry, Mountain, Armoured, Mechanised (RAPID) and Artillery divisions.
- What is an Infantry Division? :: Built around infantry brigades, suited for plains/holding operations.
- What is a Mountain Division? :: Infantry division trained and equipped for high-altitude/mountain warfare, with lighter equipment and mules/porters.
- What is an Armoured Division? :: Built around armoured brigades of tanks and mech infantry for offensive operations.
- What is an Artillery Division? :: A formation of artillery brigades providing concentrated fire support to a Corps.
- What is the difference between an Infantry Division and a Mountain Division? :: Mountain divisions are lighter, more mobile on foot, trained for high altitude; infantry divisions operate in plains.
- What is the role of a Division during operations? :: Fights the battle in its area by controlling brigades and fire support.
- What are divisional troops? :: Units directly under Division HQ – artillery, engineers, signals, air defence etc.

## Phase 1 – Army: Brigade
- What is a Brigade? :: A formation of usually three battalions plus support – about 3,000–3,500 soldiers.
- Where does a Brigade fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Between Division and Battalion.
- Who commands a Brigade? :: Brigade Commander.
- What is the rank of a Brigade Commander? :: Brigadier.
- How many battalions usually make up an Infantry Brigade? :: Three.
- Can a Brigade contain different types of battalions? :: Yes – it can have mixed battalions, especially in independent brigades.
- What is a Brigade Headquarters? :: The HQ that commands the brigade.
- What is an independent Brigade, and how is it different from a regular Brigade? :: An independent brigade has its own supporting arms and is not part of a division; a regular brigade is under a division.
- What is the role of a Brigade during an operation? :: Fights the battle in its sector with its battalions, supported by artillery and engineers.
- What supporting elements does a Brigade need? :: Artillery, engineers, signals, air defence, logistics, medical.
- What is a Brigade Group? :: A Brigade with its own supporting arms and services to fight independently. (New: Rudra all-arms brigades, 2025.)
- Is a Brigade always part of a Division? :: No – independent brigades exist.

## Phase 1 – Army: Battalion
- What is a Battalion? :: The basic fighting unit of infantry – about 800–900 soldiers, 4 rifle companies and HQ.
- Where does a Battalion fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Below Brigade, above Company.
- Who commands an Infantry Battalion? :: Commanding Officer (CO).
- What is the rank of a Battalion Commander? :: Colonel.
- How many companies are usually in an Infantry Battalion? :: Four rifle companies plus HQ/support elements.
- What is a Battalion Headquarters? :: The CO's HQ – 2IC, Adjutant, QM, Subedar Major, signal and admin elements.
- What are the major components of an Infantry Battalion? :: Bn HQ, HQ company (support platoons – mortar, anti-tank, signal, pioneer etc.), and four rifle companies.
- What is the role of the Battalion Commander? :: Commands, trains, disciplines and looks after the welfare of the whole battalion.
- What is the role of the Second-in-Command? :: Deputy to the CO; looks after administration and takes over when the CO is away.
- What is the role of the Adjutant? :: Staff officer for discipline, ceremonial, personnel and the CO's orders.
- What is the role of the Quartermaster? :: Responsible for rations, clothing, equipment, ammunition and stores.
- What is the difference between a Battalion Commander and a Company Commander? :: CO (Colonel) commands the battalion (~800); a Coy Cdr (Major) commands a company (~120).
- What is a Battalion's role during an operation? :: Captures or holds ground; fights at the tactical level within the brigade plan.
- What are the Battalion-level supporting elements? :: Mortars, anti-tank, MMG, signals, pioneers, Ghatak platoon, medical (RMO).
- What is the difference between a Battalion and a Regiment? :: Battalion is infantry's unit; in other arms (Armour, Artillery, Engineers) the equivalent unit is called a Regiment. In Infantry, "Regiment" also means the parent group of battalions (e.g. Kumaon Regiment).
- Why are some Army units called regiments and others battalions? :: Tradition: cavalry/artillery units are called regiments; infantry battalions belong to an infantry regiment.

## Phase 1 – Army: Company
- What is a Company? :: A sub-unit of a battalion – 3 platoons plus HQ, about 100–120 men.
- Where does a Company fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Below Battalion, above Platoon.
- Who commands an Infantry Company? :: Company Commander.
- What is the usual rank of a Company Commander? :: Major.
- How many platoons usually make up an Infantry Company? :: Three.
- What is a Company Headquarters? :: Company Commander, 2IC, Company Havildar Major (CHM), CQMH, signallers and runners.
- What is the role of a Company Commander? :: Leads, trains and administers the company and fights it in battle.
- What is the role of a Company Second-in-Command? :: Assists the Company Commander and takes over in his absence.
- What is the difference between a Company and a Battalion? :: A Company is a sub-unit (~120) under a Major; a Battalion is the unit (~800) under a Colonel.
- What is a Rifle Company? :: Infantry company with three rifle platoons – the basic fighting company.
- What is a Support Company? :: Company with heavier weapons – mortars, anti-tank, MMG – supporting the rifle companies.
- Can a company operate independently? :: Yes, for a limited task with support (e.g. a post, patrol base).
- What is an independent company? :: A company detached to operate on its own (e.g. a post or special task).
- What supporting weapons or elements may be available at company level? :: Mortars, MMG, rocket launchers/ATGM, sniper, radios.

## Phase 1 – Army: Platoon
- What is a Platoon? :: A sub-unit of a company – 3 sections plus HQ, about 30–35 men.
- Where does a Platoon fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Below Company, above Section.
- Who commands an Infantry Platoon? :: Platoon Commander.
- What rank usually commands a Platoon? :: Lieutenant/Captain or a JCO (Subedar/Naib Subedar).
- How many sections usually make up a Platoon? :: Three.
- What is a Platoon Headquarters? :: Platoon Commander, Platoon Havildar, radio operator and runner.
- What is the role of a Platoon Commander? :: Leads, trains and looks after his men; executes the company's tasks.
- What is the role of a Platoon Havildar? :: Senior NCO – assists the officer with discipline, administration and the men's welfare.
- What is the difference between a Platoon Commander and a Company Commander? :: Pl Cdr leads ~30 men (Lt); Coy Cdr leads ~120 men (Maj).
- What are a Platoon Commander's responsibilities during training? :: Fitness, weapon handling, firing, field craft, discipline, welfare.
- What are a Platoon Commander's responsibilities in the field? :: Plan, lead, keep men safe, accomplish the mission, report.
- What is the role of a Platoon during an operation? :: Attacks, defends, patrols, ambushes, holds a post within the company plan.
- Can a Platoon operate independently? :: Yes, for small tasks such as patrols, ambushes or a post.
- What is the approximate strength of an Infantry Platoon? :: About 30–35 soldiers.
- Why is the Platoon important for a young Army officer? :: It is where he first leads men directly and learns leadership on the ground.

## Phase 1 – Army: Section
- What is a Section? :: Smallest sub-unit of infantry – about 10 soldiers.
- Where does a Section fit in the Army hierarchy? :: Below Platoon.
- Who commands an Infantry Section? :: Section Commander.
- What is the rank of a Section Commander? :: Havildar or Naik.
- What is the approximate strength of an Infantry Section? :: About 10.
- What is the role of a Section Commander? :: Leads the section, controls fire and movement, looks after his men.
- Which weapons are normally found at Section level? :: INSAS/AK-203/SIG-716 rifles, LMG, grenades, under-barrel grenade launcher.
- What is the relationship between a Section and a Platoon? :: Three sections make a platoon.
- What is the smallest tactical sub-unit of Infantry? :: The Section.
- What is a fire team? :: A small group (4–5 men) in a section working together in fire and movement.
- How is a Section organised into fire teams? :: Usually two groups: rifle group and LMG/fire-support group.
- What is the role of a Rifleman? :: Basic infantry soldier – fights with his rifle, carries out section tasks.
- What is the role of a Light Machine Gun operator? :: Provides sustained covering fire for the section.
- What is the role of the Section Commander during an operation? :: Leads, gives fire control orders, keeps the section together, completes the task.

## Phase 1 – Army: Complete Hierarchy
- Explain the complete hierarchy from Army Headquarters down to a soldier: Army, Command, Corps, Division, Brigade, Battalion, Company, Platoon, Section. :: Army HQ → Command (Lt Gen) → Corps (Lt Gen) → Division (Maj Gen) → Brigade (Brig) → Battalion (Col) → Company (Maj) → Platoon (Lt/Capt/JCO) → Section (Hav/Naik).
- Which of these are formations and which are units? :: Formations: Command, Corps, Division, Brigade. Unit: Battalion/Regiment. Sub-units: Company, Platoon, Section.
- At which level does a Lieutenant normally command? :: Platoon (or troop).
- At which level does a Captain normally command? :: Platoon/company 2IC; sometimes a company.
- At which level does a Major normally command? :: Company.
- At which level does a Lieutenant Colonel normally command? :: Second-in-command of a battalion (CO in some cases).
- At which level does a Colonel normally command? :: Battalion/Regiment (as CO).
- At which level does a Brigadier normally command? :: Brigade.
- At which level does a Major General normally command? :: Division.
- At which level does a Lieutenant General normally command? :: Corps or Command.
- What is the relationship between rank, appointment and command? :: Rank shows seniority; appointment is the job held; command is the authority that comes with the appointment.
- Can an officer's rank and appointment be different? :: Yes – e.g. a Major can be acting CO; a Colonel can hold a staff appointment.
- Who is the immediate superior of a Platoon Commander? :: Company Commander.
- Who is the immediate superior of a Company Commander? :: Commanding Officer.
- Who is the immediate superior of a Battalion Commander? :: Brigade Commander.
- What is the difference between commanding soldiers and administering soldiers? :: Commanding = leading in training and operations; administering = looking after pay, leave, welfare, discipline and logistics.
- Why does the Army keep such a hierarchical structure? :: Clear responsibility, quick decisions, discipline and coordination in war.

## Phase 1 – Army: IO Cross-Questions
- If I make you a Lieutenant today, what exactly will you command? :: A platoon of about 30 soldiers (with a JCO and NCOs).
- How many men will you command? :: About 30–35.
- Who will be your immediate junior? :: Platoon Havildar / Section Commanders (JCO if attached).
- What will your daily responsibilities be? :: PT, training, weapon practice, checking turnout, welfare, admin, duties assigned by the Coy Cdr.
- What would your men expect from you? :: Fairness, care, competence, courage and leading by example.
- What would you expect from your JCOs? :: Experience, guidance, discipline and link with the men.
- What would you expect from your NCOs? :: Supervision of the men, discipline, setting standards.
- How would you maintain discipline in your platoon? :: Fair and consistent rules, personal example, reward and punishment.
- How would you maintain morale? :: Care for welfare, sports, recognition, communication and leading by example.
- How would you train your platoon? :: Fitness, weapon handling, field craft, drills, tactical exercises, gradually increasing difficulty.
- What would you do if one of your soldiers repeatedly failed to meet the required standard? :: Find the reason, give extra training and support; if no improvement, take formal action.
- What would you do if you disagreed with your Platoon Havildar? :: Listen to his experience; discuss privately; take the final decision and own it.
- What would you do if a soldier's personal problem was affecting his performance? :: Talk to him, help solve it (leave, welfare, help with family); keep him productive.
- What makes a good Platoon Commander? :: Competence, fairness, care for men, courage, integrity, leading by example.
- Why does a young Lieutenant need experienced JCOs and NCOs? :: They have years of experience and know the men and the terrain; they guide young officers.
- Why is trust between an officer and his men important? :: Men follow willingly into danger only if they trust their leader.
- What is the difference between authority and leadership? :: Authority is the power given by rank; leadership is the ability to make people willingly follow you.
- What does "lead from the front" mean in the Army? :: The officer leads in person, shares hardships and goes first into danger.
- What would you do if your platoon failed during a training exercise? :: Analyse, take responsibility, correct mistakes, retrain and try again.
- What would you do if your platoon performed exceptionally well? :: Appreciate and reward them publicly, and keep standards high.
- What is an Army officer's most important responsibility towards his men? :: Their welfare and safety while accomplishing the mission – "the safety, honour and welfare of the men you command come next" (Chetwode motto).
- Why do you want to be an Army officer rather than just join the Armed Forces? :: I want to lead soldiers, take responsibility and make decisions – an officer's role.
- Why SSC through OTA? :: My age (23, graduate) fits SSC; OTA's 49-week course; 10-year commitment with option for Permanent Commission.
- Why the Indian Army? :: I want a field career, leading men on the ground; my fitness and leadership suit the Army.

## Phase 2 – Arms & Services: Basics
- What is an Arm in the Indian Army? :: Fighting component – Infantry, Armoured Corps, Mech Infantry, Artillery, Engineers, Signals, Army Air Defence, Army Aviation, Intelligence Corps.
- What is a Service in the Indian Army? :: Supports and sustains the arms – ASC, AMC, EME, AOC, Army Postal Service, JAG, AEC, RVC, Military Police, Pioneer Corps etc.
- What are the combat arms of the Indian Army? :: Infantry, Armoured Corps, Mechanised Infantry.
- What are the supporting arms? :: Artillery, Engineers, Signals, Army Air Defence, Army Aviation, Intelligence Corps.
- What are the Services of the Indian Army? :: ASC, AOC, EME, AMC, ADC, Military Nursing Service, APS, JAG, AEC, RVC, Corps of Military Police, Pioneer Corps, DSC.
- Which arm is the primary arm for holding ground? :: Infantry.
- Why does the Army need multiple Arms and Services? :: Modern war needs combined arms – fighting, fire support, communication and logistics.
- How do different Arms and Services work together during an operation? :: Through combined-arms planning: artillery fires, engineers clear paths, infantry and armour assault, signals link everyone, services sustain.
- Can an officer be commissioned into any Arm or Service? :: He can give preference, but allotment depends on vacancies and merit.
- What decides the Arm or Service allotted to an officer? :: Merit (order of merit at academy), preference, medical category, vacancies, qualifications.

## Phase 2 – Infantry
- What is the role of Infantry? :: Close with and destroy the enemy, capture and hold ground.
- Why is Infantry called the basic arm of the Army? :: It is the primary arm that captures and holds ground; all others support it ("Queen of Battle").
- What are the primary tasks of Infantry? :: Attack, defend, patrol, ambush, raid, counter-insurgency.
- What is the difference between Infantry's offensive and defensive tasks? :: Offensive: attacking to capture ground. Defensive: holding positions against attack.
- Which types of terrain is Infantry particularly suited for? :: All terrain – especially mountains, jungle, built-up areas.
- What are the major Infantry regiments of the Indian Army? :: Madras, Rajputana Rifles, Rajput, Jat, Sikh, Punjab, Dogra, Garhwal Rifles, Kumaon, Maratha LI, Sikh LI, Bihar, Grenadiers, JAK Rifles, JAK LI, Mahar, Assam, Naga, Ladakh Scouts, Gorkha Rifles (1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11 GR), Parachute Regiment, Brigade of the Guards, Sikkim Scouts, Arunachal Scouts.
- What is a Regimental Centre? :: Training and administrative home of a regiment – recruits are trained there (e.g. Kumaon Regimental Centre, Ranikhet).
- What is the role of an Infantry Battalion in a Brigade? :: One of the brigade's three fighting units.
- Which weapons are commonly used by Infantry? :: Rifles (AK-203, SIG-716, INSAS), LMG, carbines, sniper rifles, mortars, ATGMs, grenades.
- Why is Infantry still important despite advances in technology? :: Only infantry can hold ground and fight in mountains, cities and among people.
- What is the role of Infantry in urban warfare? :: Clears buildings, holds key points, protects civilians.
- What is the role of Infantry in mountain warfare? :: Holds heights and passes, fights on foot in high altitude.
- What is the role of Infantry in counter-insurgency operations? :: Cordon and search, patrols, intelligence-based operations, winning hearts and minds.

## Phase 2 – Armoured Corps
- What is the role of the Armoured Corps? :: Mobile shock action and firepower – destroying enemy and capturing ground.
- What is the basic fighting platform of the Armoured Corps? :: The Main Battle Tank.
- What is an Armoured Regiment? :: A unit of tanks (about 45 tanks) commanded by a Colonel.
- How is an Armoured Regiment organised? :: Three sabre squadrons (each with troops of 3 tanks) plus HQ squadron.
- Who commands an Armoured Regiment? :: Commanding Officer (Colonel).
- What is the role of tanks in warfare? :: Firepower, protection and mobility – shock action.
- What is the difference between a tank and an Infantry Combat Vehicle? :: Tank: heavy armour and a big gun to destroy enemy; ICV: carries infantry into battle with lighter armour.
- What is a Main Battle Tank? :: A heavily armoured tank combining firepower, mobility and protection – T-90, T-72, Arjun.
- What are the major tanks operated by the Indian Army? :: T-72 (Ajeya), T-90 (Bhishma), Arjun Mk1/Mk1A; Zorawar light tank under induction.
- What is the role of armour in offensive operations? :: Breakthrough, deep strikes, exploitation.
- What is the role of armour in defensive operations? :: Counter-attacks and blocking enemy armour.
- What are the limitations of tanks? :: Heavy, need fuel and maintenance, vulnerable in mountains, cities and to ATGMs/drones.
- Why are tanks vulnerable in certain terrains? :: Mountains, marshes, forests and urban areas restrict movement.
- What is an Armoured Brigade? :: A formation of three armoured regiments with mechanised infantry.
- How does the Armoured Corps work with Infantry? :: Infantry protects tanks from close threats; tanks give firepower to infantry.
- What is a combined-arms operation? :: Infantry, armour, artillery, engineers and air working together.
- Why is mobility important for armoured forces? :: Speed lets them strike deep, surprise and exploit success.

## Phase 2 – Mechanised Infantry
- What is Mechanised Infantry? :: Infantry mounted in armoured vehicles (BMP) to move and fight alongside tanks.
- Why was Mechanised Infantry developed? :: To keep pace with tanks and survive on a modern battlefield.
- What is the difference between Mechanised Infantry and conventional Infantry? :: Mech Inf moves in armoured vehicles; conventional infantry moves on foot or trucks.
- Which vehicles does Mechanised Infantry use? :: BMP-1, BMP-2 (Sarath), NAMICA (anti-tank), WhAP.
- What is an Infantry Combat Vehicle? :: Armoured vehicle carrying infantry with its own weapons – e.g. BMP-2.
- What is the role of Mechanised Infantry in a combined-arms operation? :: Fights alongside tanks, protects them, clears positions.
- How does Mechanised Infantry support tanks? :: Clears infantry and anti-tank threats, holds captured ground.
- What are the advantages of Mechanised Infantry? :: Protected mobility, firepower, speed.
- What are the limitations of Mechanised Infantry? :: Fuel, maintenance and vulnerability to anti-tank weapons; limited in mountains.
- What is the difference between an APC and an IFV? :: APC = armoured transport; IFV = armed fighting vehicle that can fight with its troops.
- What is the role of Mechanised Infantry in offensive operations? :: Assaults with tanks and holds ground gained.

## Phase 2 – Regiment of Artillery
- What is the role of the Regiment of Artillery? :: Provides fire support to destroy, neutralise or suppress the enemy.
- Why is Artillery called the firepower arm? :: It delivers most of the firepower on the battlefield ("God of War").
- What are the main tasks of Artillery? :: Destroy enemy positions, support attacks, counter-bombardment, deny areas.
- What is field artillery? :: Guns and howitzers that support ground forces in battle.
- What is a gun? :: Long-barrelled weapon firing at high velocity, flatter trajectory.
- What is a howitzer? :: Shorter barrel, fires at higher angles – can hit targets behind hills.
- What is a rocket artillery system? :: Launches unguided/guided rockets in salvoes – e.g. Pinaka, Smerch.
- What is the difference between a gun, a howitzer and a rocket system? :: Gun: flat trajectory, high velocity. Howitzer: high angle. Rocket system: salvo fire, area saturation.
- What is an artillery regiment? :: Artillery unit of three batteries (usually 18 guns) under a Colonel.
- How does Artillery support Infantry? :: Destroys and suppresses enemy before and during the infantry attack.
- How does Artillery support armoured forces? :: Suppresses anti-tank weapons and supports armour advance.
- What is indirect fire? :: Firing at a target not visible to the gun, with observers directing it.
- What is direct fire? :: Firing at a target directly visible to the gunner.
- What is counter-bombardment? :: Destroying enemy artillery.
- What is fire support? :: Fire provided to assist manoeuvring troops.
- What is a fire plan? :: Plan of artillery targets and timings for an operation.
- What is an artillery observation post? :: Position from which an observer watches the target and directs fire.
- What is the role of a Forward Observation Officer? :: Artillery officer who joins infantry/armour and directs fire.
- What is the role of Artillery during an offensive? :: Preparatory bombardment, suppression, support to assault.
- What is the role of Artillery during a defensive operation? :: Breaks up enemy attacks, counter-bombardment, defensive fire.
- What are the major artillery systems used by the Indian Army? :: Bofors FH-77B, K9 Vajra, Dhanush, M777, ATAGS, Pinaka, Smerch, BrahMos, Pralay (inducting).
- What is the difference between artillery and mortars? :: Mortars are light, short-range, high-angle infantry weapons; artillery is heavier, longer range.
- At what level are mortars generally used? :: Battalion/company level.
- Why is accurate target acquisition important for Artillery? :: To hit the right target first time, saving ammo and avoiding friendly losses.

## Phase 2 – Corps of Engineers
- What is the role of the Corps of Engineers? :: Help own forces move, stop enemy movement, and build infrastructure.
- What are the major functions of Army Engineers? :: Bridging, mine laying/clearing, demolitions, obstacles, field fortifications, roads, water supply.
- What is combat engineering? :: Engineering in battle – mobility, counter-mobility, survivability.
- What is the role of Engineers during an offensive operation? :: Breach minefields and obstacles, bridge rivers, open routes.
- What is the role of Engineers during defensive operations? :: Lay mines, build obstacles and bunkers.
- What is a minefield? :: An area with mines laid to stop or slow the enemy.
- Why are minefields used? :: To delay and channel enemy, protect positions.
- What is mine clearance? :: Detecting and removing mines to open safe lanes.
- What is the role of Engineers in river-crossing operations? :: Build bridges, ferry troops, secure crossings.
- What is a bridge-laying operation? :: Laying a bridge, often by bridge-laying tank, so forces can cross.
- What is the role of Engineers in building obstacles? :: Ditches, mines, wire, anti-tank obstacles.
- What is the role of Engineers in disaster relief? :: Rescue, bridges, roads, clearing debris.
- What is the difference between combat engineering and civil engineering? :: Combat engineering is done under fire for war; civil engineering builds peacetime infrastructure.
- What is an Engineer Regiment? :: Engineer unit under a Colonel.
- How do Engineers support Infantry and armoured forces? :: Mobility (clearing routes), counter-mobility, protection.
- What is the role of the Corps of Engineers in peacetime? :: Builds and maintains roads, bridges, buildings (MES), Border Roads (BRO), disaster relief and training.

## Phase 2 – Corps of Signals
- What is the role of the Corps of Signals? :: Provides communication and information systems for command and control.
- Why are communications important in military operations? :: Commanders can't control forces, coordinate fire or get information without them.
- What are the major responsibilities of the Corps of Signals? :: Radio, satellite, line and data networks, electronic warfare, cyber security, network management.
- What is military communication? :: Exchange of information within the forces for command and control.
